Standing desk converter vs. full standing desk: which is right for you?

A full electric standing desk — the Flexispot E7, Uplift V2, or similar — is the premium solution for a dedicated home office setup. You get a full desk surface that raises and lowers at the touch of a button, usually with memory presets, and a completely clear workspace whether you're sitting or standing. Prices start around $400 and go up to $1,500+ for commercial-grade units.

A standing desk converter is a different product for a different situation. You place it on top of your existing desk, and it raises a portion of your workspace — typically a monitor and keyboard area — to standing height. The converter then folds or lowers back down when you want to sit. Prices are $80–$350. No installation. Nothing to assemble. They work on any flat desk surface.

The case for a converter: you rent (can't bolt things to walls or modify furniture), you already have a desk you like, you need a solution you can move or take with you, or you're not sure yet if standing while working suits you and don't want to invest $600+ to find out. Converters are the practical middle ground that gets most remote workers 80% of the ergonomic benefit at 20% of the cost.

What to look for in a standing desk converter

Surface width. Most people need at least 30 inches to fit a monitor and have a usable keyboard/mouse area. 35–48 inches is the sweet spot for most WFH setups. If you run dual monitors, prioritize converters with 47"+ platforms or a separate monitor arm.

Height range. You need the converter to reach your standing height — typically 10–16 inches above desk surface level for most people, depending on height. Check that the model you're considering reaches high enough for your standing ergonomic position (elbows bent at ~90 degrees while standing).

Lift mechanism. Pneumatic (gas-assist) lifts are the smoothest and most reliable — you push a lever and the platform rises with one hand. Spring-assisted lifts are similar. Crank-based height adjustment is cheaper but requires more effort. Electric converters (with buttons) exist but add significant cost and complexity.

Keyboard tray. Some converters include an integrated keyboard tray that sits slightly lower than the main surface — this is the ergonomic ideal. Others have the keyboard on the same level as the monitor. An integrated tray lets you position your monitor at eye level while keeping your keyboard at elbow height.

Weight capacity. Most converters handle 25–35 lbs. A single 27" monitor weighs 8–15 lbs; a dual-monitor arm setup will approach the limit. Check your monitor weight before buying.

Getting the most from your standing desk converter

Set your standing height first. Before using the converter, stand next to your desk and bend your elbows to 90 degrees. Mark that height. That's where your keyboard should be when standing — not eye level. This is the most commonly miscalibrated ergonomic setting.
Get an anti-fatigue mat. Standing on a hard floor for 30+ minutes causes foot and leg fatigue faster than most people expect. A quality anti-fatigue mat (Topo, Ergodriven, or similar) makes all-day standing sustainable. Budget $40–$80 for a good one.
Alternate, don't stand all day. The research is clear: standing all day is as hard on your body as sitting all day. Aim for 15–30 minute standing intervals, alternating with seated work. A cheap standing desk timer (or a phone reminder) keeps you alternating habitually.
Use a monitor arm if space is tight. A monitor arm lets you push your monitor back when seated and pull it forward when standing, without it taking permanent real estate on the converter platform. Adds flexibility and reclaims desk space.
Wear supportive shoes or go barefoot. Surprisingly important. Thin-soled casual shoes cause more discomfort than either supportive footwear or bare feet. If you work from home and your floors are comfortable, bare feet on a mat is often the best option.
Check your monitor at eye level. When standing, your monitor's top edge should be at or slightly below eye level. If you need to raise it, a monitor riser on the converter platform works well. If it's already too high, consider a monitor arm that can be positioned lower.

Frequently asked questions

Is a standing desk converter worth it?

Yes, especially if you already have a desk you like or can't afford a full sit-stand desk. Standing desk converters cost $100–$300 vs. $400–$1,500+ for a full electric standing desk. They set up in minutes with no assembly and work on any flat surface. For people who primarily sit and occasionally stand, a converter is the most practical and affordable solution.

How long should you stand at a standing desk?

Current ergonomic guidance recommends alternating sitting and standing rather than standing all day. A common target is 15–30 minutes of standing per hour of work. Most people find a natural rhythm of standing for 20–45 minutes, then sitting. Standing all day is actually harder on your body than a mix. The key is movement and alternation.

Will a standing desk converter damage my desk?

Most standing desk converters sit on top of your desk without attachment — they hold their position via weight. The FlexiSpot M7 weighs about 22 lbs, which creates a stable base without bolts or clamps. As long as your desk surface is flat and large enough, no damage occurs. Some models offer optional clamps for extra security on slippery surfaces.

What's the difference between a Z-lift and X-lift converter?

Z-lift converters use a Z-shaped scissor mechanism to raise the work surface — space-efficient and the most common type. X-lift converters use a broader scissor mechanism, typically more stable at maximum height but taking more desk space when lowered. Both types work well. The VIVO DESK-V000E is an X-lift style; the FlexiSpot M7 uses a Z-lift design.

Can I use a standing desk converter with dual monitors?

Yes, but check weight capacity and surface size. For dual monitors, consider the Mount-It! MI-7940 with its 47-inch wide platform and 44 lb weight capacity. A monitor arm (sold separately) can also free up surface space by raising both monitors on a single arm above the converter deck, leaving the full platform available for keyboard and accessories.
