Measure before choosing a converter.

  1. Check the base footprint.

    Measure usable desk width and depth, including cable cutouts and anything fixed to the surface. Compare the base dimensions, not just the top platform’s width. Check the manufacturer’s minimum desk requirements.

  2. Check seated as well as standing height.

    A converter adds height even when lowered. Make sure the keyboard position will still work when sitting. A design that only fits your standing setup may leave you with an awkward seated one.

  3. Separate the load limits.

    Weigh the monitor, stand or arm, laptop, and accessories. Check whether the main surface and keyboard tray have separate capacities, and whether a monitor arm is permitted. Do not treat the overall advertised capacity as a tray rating.

  4. Trace the movement and cable path.

    Verify whether the model rises vertically or also moves toward you. Allow cable slack across the full travel and keep the mechanism clear, following the manual.

  5. Check assembly, returns, and warranty.

    Some models need the keyboard tray attached. Confirm the exact manual, packaging requirements, return transport costs, and whether the seller is authorized for warranty coverage.

Why this is not a back-pain treatment claim.

We have not assessed any converter as a treatment or measured ergonomic outcomes. A change in desk height does not by itself establish comfort or health benefits. This guide addresses product fit, identifiable models, and purchase conditions.

A future hands-on comparison should document movement at full height, usable keyboard space, adjustment effort with a stated load, and the footprint in both positions. Those tests would support a recommendation more credibly than the unsupported rankings in the earlier version.
