Budget Robot Vacuum Guide: 11S & Roomba 694

Two identifiable models, their useful differences, and the checks that matter before spending a $200 budget. Older recommendations with unverified offers have been withdrawn.

Choose a model for your floor plan.

Eufy RoboVac 11S: the original 11S is the low-profile candidate here. Eufy describes a 2.85-inch body and includes a remote control. Its model comparison lists 1,300 Pa maximum suction. The earlier article incorrectly used the 2,000 Pa figure associated with the 11S Max. These are different products.

Roomba 694: iRobot describes app scheduling, Dirt Detect, two Multi-Surface Brushes, and an Edge-Sweeping Brush. Dirt Detect is described as focusing cleaning on dirtier areas. We no longer describe this as a dual-rubber-roller model or promise tangle-free pet-hair collection.

Lefant M210: the older page still appeared in search results, but a direct check returned a missing-page response. We have withdrawn its purchase recommendation rather than substitute the M210P or M210 Pro+ without re-evaluating them.

Our shortlist is conditional: compare the 11S for furniture clearance and the 694 for its stated scheduling and brush system. We have not established a cleaning winner.

Five checks that prevent a disappointing purchase.

  1. Measure under the furniture you want cleaned.

    Compare the lowest clearance with the robot’s full height. Allow space for uneven floors rather than buying on a height figure that barely fits.

  2. Match the cleaning task.

    List hard floors, rugs, carpet pile, pet hair, thresholds, and tight chair legs. A suction-pressure number does not tell you how well the robot will negotiate that combination.

  3. Verify navigation and controls.

    Check whether you need room selection, restricted zones, remote operation, or scheduled cleaning. Do not infer map-based cleaning from an “auto” mode.

  4. Distinguish returning to charge from resuming a job.

    Automatic docking alone does not establish that a robot will continue an unfinished clean. Look for an explicit statement about the exact model.

  5. Check the ownership costs.

    Find compatible filters, brushes, batteries, and the seller’s warranty terms. For older stock, confirm whether the unit is new, refurbished, or used and who handles returns.

What would justify a stronger recommendation?

A useful hands-on comparison would measure pickup on the same floor surfaces, missed areas, obstacle handling, hair cleanup, noise, and intervention time. We do not have those results. This guide now separates source-checked model information from the performance questions that remain unanswered.
