Lowest listed purchase price

The Ordinary Salicylic Acid 2% Solution.

The US listing identifies a water-based serum with 2% salicylic acid as its acne-treatment active. Its 30 mL bottle had the lowest displayed price in this comparison. That makes it the price-first option to discuss if this is already the type of product you intend to buy; it does not establish better results or suitability for your skin.

The brand warns that combining it with another topical acne medication increases the chance of dryness and irritation. Check the product's full directions and compatibility guidance before purchase.

Rinse-off cleanser

CeraVe Renewing SA Cleanser.

CeraVe's US page lists salicylic acid, niacinamide, hyaluronic acid, and three ceramides. It describes a fragrance-free cleanser that is rinsed off. The salicylic acid concentration is not stated in the checked listing, so we do not assign a percentage or treat it as interchangeable with a 2% leave-on product.

Compare this when shopping specifically for a cleanser. It is not a universal recommendation for every person with acne, and an ingredient list cannot prove that it will suit you. Verify the Renewing SA name, bottle size, package ingredients, and retailer price.

The formula correction

Effaclar BPO is a benzoyl peroxide treatment.

The current US Effaclar BPO Multi-Target Acne Treatment lists 5.5% benzoyl peroxide. It is a leave-on treatment, and the merchant warns that it may discolor fabrics. The selected 40 mL listing displayed $39.99.

The old US Effaclar Duo is listed as discontinued and also contained 5.5% benzoyl peroxide. Our earlier article incorrectly described it as an alternative to that ingredient. We have replaced that recommendation with the clearly named current BPO product. Effaclar Duo+M is a separate salicylic acid formula; the similar names are not a reason to treat them as interchangeable.

This entry is for comparing a benzoyl peroxide product's label and cost, not a recommendation to add it to the other treatments here.

Another 2% salicylic acid option

IMAGE CLEAR CELL clarifying acne spot treatment.

IMAGE lists a leave-on gel with 2% salicylic acid, priced at $38 for a one-time purchase when checked. The ingredient list also includes zinc PCA and tea tree leaf oil. Make the comparison useful

Buy for a defined need, not a four-product routine.

  1. Confirm the exact US product.

    Match the full product name, active ingredient, concentration where declared, and package size. Similar brand names can conceal materially different formulas. The package you receive is the final label to check.

  2. Separate cleanser and treatment costs.

    A rinse-off cleanser and a leave-on treatment have different roles. We have not measured dose or how long a container lasts, so this guide does not claim a cost per use. Compare delivered prices for the exact sizes you intend to buy.

  3. Read directions before combining products.

    This list is not a layering schedule. The American Academy of Dermatology advises using acne treatments as directed and avoiding practices that irritate skin. Take questions about combining treatments or choosing an active ingredient to a pharmacist or dermatologist.

  4. Keep outcome claims separate from shopping facts.

    We have verified the published formula information, not how your skin will respond. No product here earns an effectiveness ranking from this research. If you need a treatment plan, a product comparison cannot supply that assessment.

Questions before checkout.

Does under $50 mean the whole routine?

No. The comparison records individual product prices at the time of research. It does not price a complete routine, include delivery or tax, or suggest buying all four.

Are all four products salicylic acid treatments?

No. Effaclar BPO lists benzoyl peroxide. CeraVe Renewing SA is a rinse-off cleanser with an undisclosed salicylic acid percentage in the checked listing. The Ordinary and IMAGE each declare 2% salicylic acid.

Which one works best?

This research cannot establish that. We have not compared clinical outcomes or performed a user trial. The useful distinctions here are the formula, format, exact product identity, and dated purchase price.
