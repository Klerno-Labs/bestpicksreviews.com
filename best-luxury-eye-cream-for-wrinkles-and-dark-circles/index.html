The MAX eye crème: a source-based buying guide

IMAGE describes this as a moisturizing eye treatment with peptides and plant cell extracts. Here is how to read that description without mistaking it for a comparative test.

When it is worth considering

It is a candidate if you want a cream-format eye product and those advertised ingredients fit your preferences. We have no first-hand evidence about its finish, eye comfort, or a result after a set number of weeks.

Where the evidence stops

The product URL contains “stem-cell,” while the description refers to plant cell extracts. Neither wording establishes a regenerative treatment. We do not have a basis for a numerical score or a claim that it is the best choice for both wrinkles and dark circles.

Before you buy

Read the complete ingredients and directions on the current package. Consider whether you need a separate eye product and whether you can obtain a sample. Compare the quantity as well as the delivered price, and establish what the merchant accepts as a return after opening.

For skin-care tolerance questions, the American Academy of Dermatology’s product-testing guide is a useful general reference. It does not endorse this product.
