These are dated listing checks, not laboratory results. Formulas, package sizes, price and availability can change; confirm the exact product before ordering.

When it is worth considering

It belongs on a shortlist for someone seeking a serum with the ingredients the brand describes. Whether that combination adds anything to your existing routine is more useful than the old “women over 40” label.

Where the evidence stops

Plant-derived cell extracts are not evidence that a cosmetic regenerates human skin. We have not verified the finished product’s clinical outcomes, measured wrinkle changes, or compared it with less expensive alternatives. Doctor-developed marketing does not make this guide medical advice.

Before you buy

Check the complete formula and package size, then compare it with products already in your routine. Ask for the evidence behind any result claim that is decisive for you. The checked description does not provide the vitamin C concentration, so do not assume it matches another IMAGE MD serum.

For skin-care tolerance questions, the American Academy of Dermatology’s product-testing guide is a useful general reference. It does not endorse this product.

A comparison to make

IMAGE MD restoring power-C serum is a separate product whose listing specifies 20% tetrahexyldecyl ascorbate. That clearer concentration label may be relevant if vitamin C form is your deciding factor, but it does not establish superiority or equivalent tolerance.
