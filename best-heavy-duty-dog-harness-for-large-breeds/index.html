The buying decision

A back-clip option to shortlist, with fit questions to resolve.

The standard Big Ass Harness is worth investigating when you need a larger chest range and a top handle. The gallery shows its leash ring on the back. We did not find a front leash attachment on this model; do not confuse it with the separately named Big Ass Tactical Harness.

Our recommendation is conditional: confirm the measurements below with the seller before choosing a size. We have not established that this harness will prevent pulling, escape, discomfort, or injury.

Size by size

Measure the chest, then check the exact size.

The merchant instructs buyers to measure around the chest behind the front legs. Its size-guide values are below. The weight column is the merchant's fitting guidance, not a tested load limit; body shape and chest measurement still need checking.

The source has conflicting unit values. For L, the description pairs a 63 cm lower limit with 26 inches; for 2XL, it pairs a 115 cm upper limit with 42.25 inches. These are not equivalent conversions. We have reproduced the inch-based size guide rather than silently choosing a correction. Ask the seller for the actual adjustable range for your selected size, especially L or 2XL.

Several ranges overlap. A measurement in two rows does not establish which shape fits better, and we found no clear rule for choosing between them. Send your measurement and intended size to the seller and use the fitting video on the product page before ordering. We have not checked the fit on a dog.

What the listing establishes—and what it does not.

The listing describes a top handle, wide straps, a ring attachment, buckle fastening, and reflective webbing. Those are design features. The page does not provide a quantified load rating, a strength-test method, a complete material specification, or a documented count and range of adjustment points.

A back attachment and a top handle are reasons to inspect the design, not evidence that it prevents pulling or safely supports lifting a dog. We have not measured pressure distribution, wear, escape resistance, or comfort. The earlier throat-injury and strongest-dog assurances were not supported by our research.

If you need a front attachment, compare a different design.

The Ruffwear Front Range Harness is an unpaid comparison option: its manufacturer lists both a back V-ring and a reinforced chest attachment, with four adjustment points. Its largest L/XL range is 32–42 inches around the chest, so it will not cover every measurement in the Big Ass chart.

Compare it when attachment choice matters and the size range fits your dog. We have not tested either harness and cannot rank their strength, comfort, or effect on pulling.

Questions before checkout.

Is this a no-pull harness?

This guide covers the standard back-clip model. We have not tested an effect on pulling. Choose the attachment design for your intended use, and do not treat a hardware change as a behavior guarantee.

Can I choose by my dog’s weight alone?

No. The listed weight bands are guidance, not a fitted measurement. Check chest circumference, overlapping sizes, the source discrepancies above, and the seller’s fitting instructions.
