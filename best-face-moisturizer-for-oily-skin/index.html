CLEAR CELL mattifying moisturizer: a buying guide for oily skin

IMAGE positions this moisturizer around hydration and the appearance of shine. Decide whether that is the job your routine needs before buying another active product.

When it is worth considering

Shortlist it if you want a moisturizer specifically marketed for a less shiny finish. We cannot tell you how matte it feels, whether it pills under your sunscreen, or how it will behave on your skin: we have not used it.

Where the evidence stops

The CLEAR CELL name is not evidence that this moisturizer treats acne. Its stated purpose on the checked page is hydration and shine appearance. Do not infer that buying the rest of the range is necessary, or that skipping moisturizer makes everyone produce more oil.

Before you buy

Check the current ingredient list against products you already tolerate, and ask the seller about opened-product returns. Compare the amount supplied and delivered cost with a simple moisturizer you know works. A larger routine is not automatically a better one.

For skin-care tolerance questions, the American Academy of Dermatology’s product-testing guide is a useful general reference. It does not endorse this product.

A comparison to make

If dryness is the actual problem, a familiar moisturizer may be a more useful starting point than paying for a mattifying claim. For a different finish, IMAGE describes BIOME+ as an oil-serum intended to look dewy; it serves a different preference and is not a like-for-like matte moisturizer.
