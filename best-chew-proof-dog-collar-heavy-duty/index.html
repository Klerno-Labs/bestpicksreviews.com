What to look for in Dog Gear

Match the collar to your dog's strength. A big, powerful dog puts real force on a collar every single time it pulls. You want reinforced webbing and hardware rated for strong dogs, not a standard pet-store strap. This one uses reinforced nylon and a tactical buckle that can take a powerful breed leaning into it.
Check the D-ring and buckle too, not only the strap. Collars almost always fail at the hardware first. The D-ring and buckle matter as much as the webbing, because that's where the leash dumps all of your dog's pulling force. A strength-tested D-ring and a heavy-duty buckle are what you're really paying for.
Don't sacrifice comfort for strength. A rugged collar that digs into the neck just won't get used. Padded lining lets you keep something sturdy on a strong dog without rubbing it raw on longer walks. That's the whole point of a collar you can leave on all day.
Confirm the size range fits your breed. This collar is sized and adjustable for large and giant breeds. Measure your dog's neck and make sure it lands inside the adjustable range before you order. The toughest collar in the world doesn't help if it doesn't actually fit.
Weigh the warranty. Any brand can call its collar durable. A lifetime warranty (like the one here) is a much better signal, because it means the brand is betting the collar lasts and will replace it if it doesn't.
Add visibility for low-light walks. If you're out before sunrise or after dark, reflective stitching helps drivers spot your dog. It's a small thing, but it matters a lot for big dogs walked near roads.
